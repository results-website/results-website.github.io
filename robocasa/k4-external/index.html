How the motion-difference score works

For each pair, subtract each point's own starting position, take the difference between their 2D displacements at each future time, and compute the root-mean-square length in native 128 px image coordinates. Identical motion has score zero even when the points start far apart. The robot–object score is the minimum across every robot–object pair. The all-pairs score is the minimum across all six pairs. The nearest-at-start score uses the robot–object pair with the smallest initial point separation.

All comparisons use the available actual horizon, up to 32 steps. Actual scores require both points visible on their surfaces and inside the image; forecast scores require both projected points inside the image. Each pair needs at least four future samples and at least half the available horizon. Actual + forecast uses the lower of their two RMS differences on the same valid future samples. A minimum is unavailable if any required pair lacks enough samples; these views remain browsable with the cutoffs at zero. Pair tables and aligned motion plots below each card make the ranking inspectable.
